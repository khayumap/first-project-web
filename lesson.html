What are Minerals and Rocks?

Rocks are naturally occurring hard substances found all around us. Most rocks are made up of one or more minerals. Rocks that contain valuable minerals in large amounts are called ores.

public The Three Layers of Planet Earth

The Earth is made of three core spherical layers:

Layer 1 The Crust

Topmost solid layer where we live. Rocks form the entire crust under soil.

Layer 2 The Mantle

Middle layer filled with intense heat where rock melts into molten magma.

Layer 3 The Core

Deepest central core under immense pressure and extreme temperatures.

explore

Where are rocks found? Rocks exist everywhere—from mountain peaks and stony riverbeds down to deep seabed trenches and underground caverns.

2

Igneous Rocks (The Fire Rocks)

local_fire_department Latin: ‘Ignis’ = Fire

Deep inside the Earth's mantle, rocks melt due to scorching temperatures. Melted molten rock is called magma. During volcanic eruptions, magma bursts onto the Earth's surface where it is known as lava. As magma or lava cools and solidifies, it crystallizes into igneous rock.

hourglass_bottom Slow Cooling (Underground)

Magma cools very slowly beneath the Earth's crust, allowing large, visible crystalline grains to form (e.g. Granite).

ac_unit Rapid Cooling (Surface)

Lava explodes out onto the surface and freezes rapidly, creating tiny crystals or trapped air holes (e.g. Pumice, Obsidian).

Granite

Slow-Cooled Magma

Formed beneath the Earth's crust. Features very large interlocking grains and great physical hardness.

Everyday Uses: Kitchen countertops, polished flooring tiles, monuments.

Pumice

Lightweight & Porous

Formed when frothy lava cools quickly with trapped air bubbles. So light with sponge-like holes that it floats on water!

Everyday Uses: Body scrubber for feet, cosmetic exfoliation, lightweight concrete.

Basalt

Dense Dark Lava

Formed by the rapid cooling of basaltic lava flows. Dense, heavy, fine-grained, dark-colored igneous rock.

Everyday Uses: Widely crushed for road gravel, asphalt mix, and building foundations.

Obsidian

Natural Volcanic Glass

Lava freezes instantaneously into shiny black rock with no visible crystal grains and razor-sharp edges.

Everyday Uses: Surgical scalpel blades, cutting tools, decorative ornaments & jewelry.
3

Sedimentary Rocks (Layered Rocks)

Wind, rain, and rushing rivers wear down rock masses into small particles called sediments. These pebbles, grains of sand, and mud travel downstream and settle in layers at the bottom of lakes, riverbeds, and ocean floors.

history_edu

Fossils Inside Sedimentary Layers

Over millions of years, the tremendous weight of upper layers presses the lower layers together until they solidify. Dead sea creatures, shells, and swamp plants get compressed between the sediment sheets, leaving behind ancient rock imprints known as fossils.

1. Sandstone

Made from tiny grains of sand cemented together over epochs. Feels gritty and comes in yellow, brown, red, or pink.

fort Historic Delhi Red Fort is built of red sandstone!
2. Limestone & Chalk

Formed primarily from calcite, derived from broken sea shells and skeletons of microscopic ocean creatures.

Key Uses: Classroom chalk and raw ingredient for cement.
3. Shale

Formed directly from hardened clay and silty mud. Incredibly smooth texture that easily separates into flat layers.

Key Uses: Fired into tiles, pottery, and construction bricks.
4. Conglomerate

Consists of rounded river pebbles, gravel, and coarse sand naturally cemented together. Resembles bumpy rough concrete.

Key Uses: Polished for decorative landscaping & heavy construction.

Metamorphic Rocks (Transformed Rocks)

The word metamorphic originates from Greek words meaning ‘to change form’.

When existing igneous or sedimentary rocks become buried deep within the crust, tremendous subterranean heat, crushing pressures, and chemical reactions fundamentally alter their internal minerals into entirely brand-new rock varieties.

Slate

Heat & Pressure

Parent Rock: Shale

Hard, smooth rock that cleanly splits into paper-thin, durable layers without shattering.

Uses: School blackboards, roof slates, garden stepping stones.

Quartzite

Extreme Heat

Parent Rock: Sandstone

Exceptionally hard, weather-resistant crystalline rock made of fused quartz grains.

Uses: Manufacturing glass, ceramics, and railway ballast.

Marble

Recrystallized

Parent Rock: Limestone

Can be carved smoothly and polished to a dazzling, glass-like lustrous shine.

Uses: The magnificent Taj Mahal, statues, palace flooring.

Gneiss

High Pressure

Parent Rock: Granite

Displays distinct, undulating alternating light and dark bands due to segregated minerals.

Uses: Architectural monuments, sculptures, road paving.

Common Minerals & Everyday Uses

Minerals are pure chemical compounds that compose all rocks. Every modern item—from kitchen salt to high-tech wristwatches—originates from minerals mined from the Earth.

Mineral Name Classification Practical Everyday Application
Iron Metallic Ore (Hematite) Steel bridges, construction beams, vehicles, nails, kitchen knives
Copper Metallic Ore Electrical wiring, plumbing pipes, cooking pots, coins
Gold & Silver Precious Metals Fine jewelry, electronic microchips, dental fillings, medals
Diamond Hardest Natural Mineral Gemstone jewelry, industrial heavy rock drills & glass-cutting tips
Quartz Silicate Crystal Precision quartz wristwatches, radio oscillators, glass manufacturing
Talc Softest Mineral (Mohs 1) Talcum baby powder, cosmetics, crayons, ceramic glaze
Halite (Rock Salt) Evaporite Mineral Daily table seasoning, food preservation, highway de-icing

Fossil Fuels: Coal & Petroleum

oil_barrel Treasures from Ancient Earth

Coal and petroleum are not minerals, but they are found deep inside the Earth's crust. They are known as fossil fuels because they were formed from the decayed remains of prehistoric plants and animals that lived hundreds of millions of years ago.

fireplace

Coal

Ancient Swamp Forests

Formed from the remains of dense swamp vegetation that died 300 million years ago. Layers of mud, silt, and rock covered them, slowly applying pressure and underground heat that transformed peat into hard black coal.

Primary uses: Generating electricity in thermal power stations, blast furnaces for steel manufacturing, and cooking fuel.
local_gas_station

Petroleum (Crude Oil)

Ancient Marine Organisms

Formed from microscopic sea creatures and algae that died and sank to the seabed. Trapped under sand and clay without oxygen, intense pressure turned their oily remains into dark, viscous liquid crude oil.

Refined into: Petrol, Diesel, Kerosene, LPG cylinder cooking gas, lubricating engine oils, and plastics.
